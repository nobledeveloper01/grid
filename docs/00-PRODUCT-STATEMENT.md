# Grid — Product Statement

**Electricity accountability and load management for Nigerian households and landlords.**

---

## The Problem

Nigerian electricity consumers have no evidence. That is the whole problem in one sentence.

A distribution company (DisCo) sends a bill. The consumer believes it is wrong. The
consumer has no meter reading history, no outage log, no consumption baseline, and no
document that a regulator will accept. So the bill gets paid, or the consumer is
disconnected. There is no third option.

This manifests in four distinct, painful ways:

### 1. Estimated billing on unmetered connections
Roughly half of registered electricity customers in Nigeria are unmetered and billed on
estimate. The estimate is generated by the DisCo, for the DisCo, with no consumer-visible
methodology. A customer who travelled for two months still receives a full bill. A customer
who had power for six hours a day is billed as though they had twenty-four.

### 2. Service-band misclassification
The Service-Based Tariff regime prices electricity by band, where the band is defined by
guaranteed daily supply hours. Band A customers pay a substantially higher rate in exchange
for a minimum of 20 hours per day. When supply falls short of the band commitment, the
customer is entitled to redress. Almost nobody claims it, because almost nobody is logging
hours of supply with timestamps.

### 3. Shared-meter allocation in multi-tenant buildings
An enormous share of urban Nigerian housing is multi-tenant compounds on a single meter.
The landlord receives one bill and divides it. The division method is: guesswork, seniority,
or whoever argues loudest. Tenants with one fan subsidise tenants with a freezer and two
air conditioners. This is a recurring source of genuine conflict.

### 4. Prepaid units running out unannounced
Prepaid customers have no burn-rate forecast. Units finish at 11pm on a Sunday. The
consumer has no way to know it was coming, and no data on which appliance is eating the
units.

---

## Why Existing Solutions Do Not Work

**DisCo apps** exist, but they are billing portals. They tell you what you owe. They do not
help you contest it, and they have an obvious structural conflict of interest in doing so.

**Smart meters** solve the metering problem but not the accountability problem, and rollout
economics mean the majority of consumers will not have one for years. Grid must work for a
consumer with an analogue meter, or no meter at all.

**Spreadsheets and WhatsApp** are what landlords actually use today. They do not produce a
defensible document, they do not survive the landlord changing phones, and they do not
generate a dispute pack.

**Home-energy monitoring products** from developed markets assume stable grid supply, a
single-family dwelling, always-on internet, and a customer who owns their electrical panel.
Every one of those assumptions is wrong here.

---

## The Product

Grid is a consumer-side evidence layer for electricity.

It does four things:

1. **Records** — meter readings via camera OCR, supply availability via automatic
   detection, and appliance inventory via guided setup.
2. **Explains** — turns that record into consumption trends, cost forecasts, band
   compliance scoring, and appliance-level load attribution.
3. **Allocates** — splits a shared meter bill across tenants fairly, by sub-reading or by
   modelled load, and produces a per-tenant statement the tenant can see.
4. **Disputes** — assembles readings, timestamps, photographs and outage logs into a
   formatted complaint pack for the DisCo and, on escalation, the regulator.

The product thesis is that the fourth capability is only credible because of the first
three. Nobody will trust a dispute letter that is not backed by a continuous, timestamped,
photograph-anchored record. Grid's moat is the accumulated history, not the letter template.

---

## Target User

**Primary — the metered urban householder.** Owns or rents a self-contained flat, has a
prepaid or postpaid meter, pays between ₦15,000 and ₦120,000 monthly, and believes — often
correctly — that the bill is wrong. Smartphone-literate. Motivated by money.

**Secondary — the compound landlord or estate manager.** Manages between 4 and 40 units on
shared or sub-metered supply. Currently allocating bills manually. Motivated by ending the
monthly argument, and by not personally absorbing the shortfall.

**Tertiary — the tenant on a shared meter.** Has the least power and the most grievance.
Onboards through the landlord. Motivated by transparency: seeing how the number was reached.

---

## Why Now

- **Tariff sensitivity is at a peak.** The Band A reclassification made electricity cost a
  front-page domestic issue. Consumers who never thought about tariff bands now know their
  band and dispute it.
- **The regulatory hook exists.** Service-band commitments create a defined, published
  standard against which supply can be measured. A consumer with a log has an actual claim.
  Grid converts a legal right that is theoretically available into one that is practically
  exercisable.
- **On-device OCR became good enough and free.** Reading a scratched analogue meter face
  under bad light no longer needs a server round-trip.
- **Solar and inverter adoption has passed the tipping point in the target segment.** That
  creates a second, richer telemetry source — and a second reason to open the app daily.

---

## The Wedge

Prepaid balance forecasting.

It is the smallest, most immediately useful thing Grid does, it requires nothing but a
camera and thirty seconds, and it produces a notification the user genuinely wants
("your units finish in 3 days"). It builds the reading history as a side effect. The
history is what makes allocation and disputes possible later.

We do not lead with disputes. Disputes are the destination, not the door.

---

## Success, Defined

Grid works if a user who has been billed unfairly can, in under five minutes, produce a
document that makes the DisCo back down — and if the user had already been using the app
for three months before they needed it, without thinking about disputes at all.

---

## Explicit Non-Goals

- **Grid does not pay bills.** It links out to whatever the user already uses. Payment is a
  commodity and owning it invites a regulatory burden with no strategic return.
- **Grid does not sell electricity units.** Same reasoning.
- **Grid is not a DisCo integration.** It works entirely from consumer-side observation.
  Any future DisCo API access is upside, never a dependency. The product must be fully
  functional against an adversarial or absent utility.
- **Grid does not control appliances.** No smart plugs, no relays, no switching. Observation
  only. Actuation is a hardware business with a hardware business's margins and support
  burden.
